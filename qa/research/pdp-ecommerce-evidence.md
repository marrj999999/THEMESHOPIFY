# PDP best-practice benchmark — high-ticket DIY kit on Shopify (BBC bamboo frame kits, £375–£795, ~80% international, social enterprise)

Compiled 2026-09-02 from free/public sources. Numbers are quoted as the source gives them; "vendor claim" flags self-reported or secondary stats. Priority column = relevance to a £375–£795 build-it-yourself kit sold DTC from the UK to a mostly international audience.

Priority key: **MUST** = missing this measurably loses sales or causes returns/support load · **SHOULD** = strong evidence, do after the musts · **NICE** = helpful, lower or vendor-only evidence.

## Notes on evidence quality
- Baymard percentages are from their benchmark of leading US/EU sites (share of sites failing a guideline), not conversion lifts. Their "Product Page UX 2026" article is the current umbrella (10 practices, updated Mar 2026).
- NN/G's PDP guidelines are free in the 2019 article; the detailed reports (Vol. 03 Product Pages, Vol. 09 Trust) are paid — topics listed only.
- Spiegel (Northwestern) is the one properly-sourced reviews-to-conversion study; the "270%/380%" figures come from a single retailer (Hammacher Schlemmer).
- Shopify/Klarna/BNPL/sticky-ATC/video lifts are vendor or secondary — directionally useful, don't quote as fact.
- IKEA-effect paper is peer-reviewed; its key boundary condition (no completion → no effect) is the actionable point for a kit seller.
- Cycle to Work: check with each provider whether a *frame kit* (not a complete bike) is accepted; Cyclescheme's public rule covers "components and parts… groupsets, wheels" so a frame is plausibly eligible, but confirm before advertising.
