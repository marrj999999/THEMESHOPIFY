## Research: schools pages (14 fetched, 1 Aug 2026)

**Reference band sequence** that converting pages agree on:
hero with offer + qualifying facts (age/key stage/price) → audience router → credibility strip →
eligibility → what it is → curriculum fit *with an honest scope limit* → compliance block
(DBS/RA/insurance) → logistics split (what we bring vs what you provide) + group size as
arithmetic → pricing as its own band → booking as numbered steps with paperwork inside a step →
dated evidence + segmented FAQ → downloadable pack.

**What a teacher needs, in order:** is this my key stage (first 50 words) → how much of MY time →
what must I arrange → will paperwork arrive without chasing → can I try it free → can I forward
a PDF to my head → does a real person answer.

**The sector-wide gap = BBC's opportunity.** Teacher time commitment, "what the school provides",
and a downloadable pack are missing from nearly every page audited. Closing them differentiates
rather than catches up. Only Greenpower states time ("around 15 hours to build").

**Best-in-class:** Hyett Education (price in the headline, compliance in one band, booking step 3
= "receive risk assessment"), Into Film (whole body written as the teacher's own questions,
eligibility first), Arkwright/Smallpeice (downloadable assembly PowerPoint so the teacher can
sell it internally).

**Proving impact without overclaiming — 4 techniques:** scope the claim down in writing
(Hyett: "no pupil workshop teaches or assesses a complete programme of study") · attribute and
date every number · prefer counts of activity over outcome claims · let named people carry the
qualitative claim (STEM publishes 8 raw teacher quotes including a lukewarm one).

## Research: university pages (16 fetched)

**Two pages, not one.** Evidence: RSA runs schools and HE as separate programmes with separate
fees, artefacts and actors · EWB's Design Challenge is university-only and gains force from it ·
**counter-example**: Ellen MacArthur merged schools + HE into one overview — result is 422 words,
no ask, and the dedicated universities URL now 301s away. Merging degraded it to nothing.
Countervailing: RSA is consolidating into "RSA Spark" — one data point the other way, weaker.

**What makes an academic take the meeting:** a named accreditation/credit framework you map to
(EWB cites AHEP4; Enactus offers an ECTS route) · evidence you know who decides (Enactus: "at the
discretion of lecturers and deans of studies") · low prep burden stated plainly · named peer
institutions · endorsement from bodies they answer to · something published and independent ·
fit to the academic year a year ahead · explicit scope boundaries.

**Rigour without a research department:** borrow a published external standard and cite it
properly → **this is exactly BBC's Swansea/BS ISO 22157:2019 data** (84.34 MPa ultimate tensile,
70.96 flexural, 6,402.93 MPa Young's modulus). Claims-Register approved *provided the standard is
cited at the point of claim*. Never place it beside "28,000 PSI" or "stronger than steel" — the
audience best able to falsify those is the audience being addressed.

**Anti-pattern BBC currently commits:** universities as a logo wall. The equivalent page in the
study (Efficiency for Access "Academic and Industry Partners") is 343 words and 36 images with no
offer, ask or contact — the page whose title best matched the need was the emptiest in the set.

**Unclaimed differentiator:** not one of the 16 partner-side pages stated an IP, ethics,
confidentiality or data position. Every such term came from the *university's* own page.

## Lessons from this session

**The recurring pattern — capability exists, connection missing.** Four times: milestone/value
blocks had no image setting (read as a content gap) · five sections had no `story` block type
(read as "nobody wrote case studies") · 143 films invisible because they live in article body
HTML · a full curriculum in the instructor app the website doesn't know about. **When something
looks like a content gap, check whether the thing can even hold the content.**

**A measurement is a hypothesis.** ~10 confident findings this session were wrong: a "527-word
hero" was the whole page total (real: 38) · "18/18 images soft" was a Playwright DPR artifact ·
a "broken mosaic" was `locator.screenshot()` failing to paint lazy images (`qa/shot.mjs` now
wraps the method that works) · three guessed CSS class names each caused a silent failure.

**Do not fix per-page what belongs to the content.** Taro's wrong image was "fixed" three times
on three pages because the override lived with the page, not the story.

**Look at every image.** A script applied article images across 7 pages and pulled in identifiable
people beside a named prison wall. `bbc-rd-prison-empty.jpg` is misnamed — it is bamboo tubes.
