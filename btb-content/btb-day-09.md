# Day 9: Assembly & Safety Testing

## SESSION OVERVIEW

The frame is finished. Today learners fit all components and carry out a full safety check against BS EN 14765 — the same standard used for bikes sold in shops. The bike does not pass until the checklist is complete. This is the standard, not a suggestion.

## BS EN 14765 Safety Checklist (Key Items)

| # | Check Item | Standard Requirement | Pass / Fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Frame integrity | No cracks, splits, or delamination visible at any joint or tube |  |
| 2 | Sharp edges | No sharp edges or burrs accessible to the child. All cut ends capped or smoothed |  |
| 3 | Handlebar grip retention | Grips cannot be pulled off by hand. Must resist 70N withdrawal force |  |
| 4 | Handlebar height | Top of handlebar must not exceed maximum height as specified. Must be locked securely |  |
| 5 | Fork and wheel security | Wheels must not be removable without tools. Axle nuts tightened to specification |  |
| 6 | Wheel rotation | Both wheels rotate freely. No contact with frame or fork at any point |  |
| 7 | Saddle security | Saddle cannot rotate or withdraw under specified load. Post correctly inserted to minimum insertion mark |  |
| 8 | Seat height | Seat height appropriate to target child. Both feet flat on floor at minimum seat height |  |
| 9 | Protrusions and trapping hazards | No protrusions over 8mm. No finger, hair, or clothing trapping points accessible to child |  |
| 10 | Stability | Bike stands unsupported or leans safely. No tendency to tip forward or back when loaded |  |
| 11 | Frame rigidity | No flex or movement at any joint under normal loading. Frame does not creak or click |  |
| 12 | Finish condition | No sharp paint edges. Lacquer intact. No bare bamboo exposed at stress points |  |

## Key Terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| BS EN 14765 | The British and European safety standard for children's bicycles. Tests cover strength, stability, and hazards |
| Torque | A twisting force. Bolts must be tightened to a specific torque to be secure without damaging the component |
| Withdrawal force | The force needed to pull something out. Grips must resist a minimum withdrawal force to pass the standard |
| Protrusion | Something sticking out. Standards limit how far anything can stick out to prevent snagging on clothing or injuring a child |
| Minimum insertion | How far a post or component must be inserted to be safe. Usually marked on the component — do not insert below the mark |
| Dropout spacing | The width between the frame ends where the wheel axle sits. Must match the wheel axle width |

## Safety Reminders

> **SAFETY — Day 9**
> 
> Safety glasses on when using the headset press or mallet — headset cups can chip
> Torque is not a suggestion — under-torqued bolts come loose; over-torqued bolts strip threads or crack bamboo
> If a component is damaged or incorrect, do not fit it. Report and replace
> Do not force anything. If it does not fit easily, stop and investigate before applying force
> All tool counts completed before and after assembly — small fasteners can go missing
> The safety checklist is a legal-level document — only sign it if the item genuinely passes

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | Standards Briefing (9:30–10:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Explain what BS EN 14765 is: a British and European safety standard for children's bicycles. It exists to ensure bikes sold to families are safe.

- Explain that the bikes they are building will be checked against this standard. If the bike does not pass, it does not get handed over.

- Walk through the 12 checklist items. Ask learners which they think will be hardest to pass on a hand-built bamboo bike.

- Learners write: “What is BS EN 14765 and why does it matter?” — one paragraph in workbook.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Every bike sold in a shop has been tested against this standard. Your bike will be tested against it too. Not because it's a legal requirement in this setting — because it is the right thing to do.”

- “If a check fails, we fix it. That's the point of the check. We do not sign off something that is not safe.”

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Large-print checklist available

- For learners with literacy difficulties: read checklist items aloud together; learner confirms understanding verbally

### Block 2 | Component Inspection (10:00–10:30, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Lay out all components for each learner's bike. Learner checks against the component list.

- Visual inspection: any damaged threads, cracked components, missing parts? Record in workbook.

- Confirm wheel dropout spacing matches frame.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Check everything before you fit anything. A damaged bolt found before fitting is easy to replace. Found after, it's a problem.”

### Block 3 | Assembly — Fork, Headset, Handlebars (10:30–11:30, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- One instructor per 2–3 learners during assembly. Constant presence needed.

- Headset: press cups into head tube with headset press. Check seated fully before fork insertion. Instructor verifies.

- Fork: insert fork through headset bearing. Fit crown race. Thread fork nut to hand-tight, then torque to specification.

- Handlebars: insert stem into fork steerer. Set height to specification. Tighten stem clamp bolts to torque. Fit grips and check they cannot be pulled off by hand.

- Instructor records observation at each sub-step.

**What To Look For**

- Headset cups not fully seated — check for even contact all round

- Handlebars not centred — check before tightening

- Grips that slide on — grips must be a secure fit, not just pushed on

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with hand strength difficulties: instructor operates the press, learner directs

- Step-by-step assembly card available with diagrams

### Block 4 | Assembly — Wheels, Seat Post, Saddle (11:45–12:45, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- Rear axle and wheels: fit rear wheel into frame dropouts. Axle through both sides. Fit nuts and washers correctly. Torque to specification. Spin wheel — check for free rotation and no frame contact.

- Front wheel: same process in fork dropouts.

- Seat post: insert to correct height for target child (from Day 1 measurements). Check minimum insertion mark is inside the tube. Tighten seat clamp bolt to torque.

- Saddle: fit and level. Tighten to torque.

**What To Look For**

- Seat post inserted below minimum insertion line — structural failure risk

- Wheel touching frame when spun — axle not centred in dropout

- Axle nuts not torqued — wheels must not be removable without tools

### Block 5 | BS EN 14765 Safety Inspection (1:45–3:00, 75 min)

**What To Do**

- Each learner works through the 12-item checklist on their own completed bike.

- Instructor conducts a parallel inspection on the same bike. Compare findings.

- Any item rated FAIL: correct the issue, re-inspect, and re-record before moving to the next item.

- Both learner and instructor sign the completed checklist.

- No bike is signed off with any fail items outstanding.

**Example of What To Say**

- “You are checking this bike. Not me. I am checking alongside you. If you find a problem, that is good — that is the point of the check.”

- “Sign your name to this only when you believe the bike is safe for a child to ride. That is the standard.”

**What To Look For**

- Learners who race through the checklist without actually checking

- Learners who are reluctant to mark something as fail — remind them: we fix it, that's the point

- Any item that cannot be fixed today — flag for start of Day 10

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Read each checklist item aloud together for learners with literacy difficulties

- Allow learner to give verbal pass/fail with instructor scribing if writing is a barrier

## Assembly Sequence

| Step | Component | Torque Check | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rear wheel | Axle nuts torqued to spec — confirm wheel does not move laterally | Check wheel spins true — no significant wobble |
| 2 | Front wheel | Quick release or axle nuts — confirm secure before next step | Check wheel spins true |
| 3 | Headset / fork | Headset bearing preload correct — no play, turns smoothly | Hold wheel, push forward — no movement in headset |
| 4 | Handlebars | Stem bolt or clamp — confirm handlebars cannot rotate or slip down | Hands on bars — push forward and back — no movement |
| 5 | Saddle / seatpost | Seatpost clamp — saddle at correct height per design sheet, confirm secure | Push down on saddle — no slip or rotation |
| 6 | Grips | Press on firmly — confirm seated to handlebar end | No sharp handlebar end exposed beyond grip |

## Differentiation

#### FOR LOW LITERACY

The BS EN 14765 checklist is pass/fail. Oral performance of each check is the primary evidence route. You write the results as they call them. They sign at the end.

#### FOR ADHD

Assembly has natural step breaks — use them. One step, instructor check, next step. The torque check is a concrete moment of completion. The safety inspection is a structured sequence — use the numbered list to anchor the process.

#### FOR ALL LEARNERS

Child safety framing works here. The standard is not bureaucratic — it exists because children fall off bikes. "This is what we check to make sure no one gets hurt." Most learners will engage seriously with this once they understand the reason.
