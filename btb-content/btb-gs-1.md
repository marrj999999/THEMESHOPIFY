# GS-1: Programme Overview — Build to Bond

## PROGRAMME OVERVIEW

> **Build to Bond**  |  **Level 1 OCN**  |  **10 Days**  |  **Instructor Reference Guide**
> This document summarises the full Build to Bond programme for instructor reference. It covers the programme rationale, key principles, assessment overview, and daily sequence.

## Programme Rationale

Build to Bond gives men in closed prison workshops the opportunity to build a bamboo balance bike for a child who matters to them. The programme uses craft-focused emotional framing: every decision — size, shape, colour, finish — is made with that child in mind.

This is not therapy language. It is practical craft work with a specific, meaningful outcome. The discipline of building something safe for someone else is the emotional engine of the programme.

## Key Principles for Instructors

- **Craft first.** The quality of the build matters. Standards are not decorative — they are the point.

- **Child safety is the standard.** BS EN 14765 is not optional. Every bike must pass before it is discussed as a gift.

- **Evidence without photography.** All evidence is written: workbook, oral Q&A, instructor observation notes.

- **SEND adaptations are built in.** Every block has explicit adaptations. Use them as starting points, not exhaustive lists.

- **Framing matters.** The language in each day's teaching sequence is deliberate. Read the “What To Say” sections before each session.

## Contingency Planning — Programme Level

#### IF A LEARNER MISSES A SESSION

- The programme is designed to run sequentially. Missing Day 5 (bonding) is the most critical gap.

- If a learner misses Day 5: their frame can be bonded by instructor with learner directed observation on the next available day. Evidence: written observation record and verbal Q&A.

- If a learner misses 2 or more days: assess whether the evidence portfolio can still be completed within the remaining sessions. If not, flag to OCN centre contact early.

#### IF THE PROGRAMME IS INTERRUPTED

- Frames can be held safely in the jig between sessions indefinitely.

- Bonded frames (after Day 5) can be stored for up to 2 weeks without affecting quality if kept dry.

- Partially sanded or painted frames: inspect before resuming. Any contamination or damage: re-sand and re-prime affected areas.

#### IF A BIKE FAILS THE SAFETY STANDARD

- Identify the specific fail item. Most can be rectified at assembly (torque, seat height, grip security).

- Structural failures (cracked joint, delamination): assess for repair. If repair is not possible, the frame cannot be issued. Document fully.

- A learner whose frame fails through no fault of their own still achieves the qualification. The evidence of the process is the qualification, not the final product.

## Reading and Literacy — Baseline Assumption

> Assume 65% functional illiteracy as the starting baseline. All materials must work at a Year 4–5 reading level (age 9–10). Visual-first, oral assessment as primary route. Written entries are secondary. Scribed responses are fully valid. Never require a learner to read aloud in front of others.

## SEND Baseline

| Need | Estimated Prevalence | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional illiteracy | ~65% | Oral primary, scribed entries, visual-first materials |
| ADHD (diagnosed or not) | ~25% | One task at a time, physical anchors, frequent check-ins |
| Anxiety | Variable | Predictable structure, physical work as anchor |
| Emotional responses to child content | Variable | Keep grounded in the practical. The bike is the intervention. |

## Emotional Framing — Key Principle

> "Build something safe for your child" is the intervention — not feelings work. Men in this context often resist anything that feels like therapy. Lead with the craft. The bike is the thing. Emotional responses are acknowledged briefly and contained in the work. Buy-in typically arrives Days 3–4 when the frame takes shape and the work becomes real.

## Gang Dynamics — Briefing Note

> Before the cohort begins, confirm with the wing manager whether any learners have known gang affiliations or conflicts with each other. Child-focused framing is generally protective — making something for a child is acceptable within most masculine identity frameworks. If rival affiliations are confirmed, notify your line manager before delivery. Do not raise with learners.
