# Day 3: Shaping & Initial Fitting

## SESSION OVERVIEW

Today the frame starts to take shape. Learners profile the joint ends of each tube so the pieces fit together tightly with no gaps. A sloppy joint fails. A tight joint holds. This is patient, precise work.

## Key Terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Profile | The shape at the end of a tube that allows it to fit snugly against another tube at a joint |
| Dry-fit | Putting the frame together without any glue to check the joints fit correctly before bonding |
| Joint gap | Any space between two bamboo surfaces at a joint. Gaps mean the joint is weaker. Maximum allowed: 1mm |
| Surform | A rasping tool used to shape and remove bamboo material. The teeth cut on the forward stroke |
| Jig | A frame that holds all the tubes in the correct position while you work. Without it, the frame geometry goes wrong |
| Geometry | The angles and positions of all the frame parts relative to each other. Geometry affects how the bike handles and whether it is safe |

## Safety Reminders

> **SAFETY — Day 3 Non-Negotiables**
> 
> Dust masks on throughout all shaping — bamboo and wood dust is a respiratory hazard
> Safety glasses on throughout — surform teeth can flick material
> Surform is a cutting tool — on the tool register, treated the same as a saw
> Never shape towards your hand — always work away from the body
> Clear swarf and dust from bench regularly — bamboo dust builds up fast
> Jig is a structural fixture — do not modify or adjust jig geometry without instructor authorisation

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | Shaping Briefing & Demo (9:30–10:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Hold up a pre-profiled tube end and an un-profiled one. Pass both round.

- Demo on scrap bamboo: how to hold the surform, stroke direction, how much to remove, how to check the fit.

- Show a good joint (tight, no light visible) and a bad joint (visible gap). Ask learners: “Which one would you trust on a child's bike?”

- Remind learners: “You can always take more off. You can't put it back. Work slowly.”

**Example of What To Say**

- “A gap in a joint is a place where the bond is weaker. One good crash and a gappy joint can fail. We want zero gaps, or as close as possible.”

**What To Look For**

- Learners who rush the demo and go straight to their own work — pause them

- Questions about angle of cut — these show good spatial thinking

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Tactile learners: let them feel the good and bad joint before the demo

- Visual instructions card showing surform grip and stroke direction available

### Block 2 | Profiling Tube Ends (10:00–11:30, 90 min)

**What To Do**

- Each learner profiles in order: head tube socket on down tube first. Most complex joint — do it when concentration is highest.

- Instructor circulates constantly. Check each joint against the mating tube before learner moves on.

- Use the 1mm card strip test: slide it round the joint. If it passes anywhere, more shaping needed.

- Final 10 minutes of block: use 120-grit to smooth all profiled ends.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Check, shape, check again. Check more than you shape. The checking is the job.”

**What To Look For**

- Over-shaping — too much removed makes the joint loose and introduces gaps

- Shaping the wrong end — make sure learners know which end to profile

- Fatigue after 45 minutes — shaping is tiring. Short breaks at the bench are fine

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with hand strength difficulties: file instead of surform for finer control

- Pair with more confident learner for checking phase if learner cannot self-assess gap size

### Block 3 | Dry-Fit in Jig (11:45–12:45, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- Learners place all tubes in their frame jig in the correct positions.

- Walk round each frame with the learner: check every joint, record gap measurements in workbook dry-fit table.

- Mark joints that need re-profiling with a small pencil dot.

- Photograph equivalent: instructor writes a brief description of each joint in the observation notes (no photos allowed).

**Example of What To Say**

- “Look at the bike in the jig. It looks like a bike. That shape is what you're going to give to someone's child. Take a moment to look at it.”

**What To Look For**

- Frame sitting correctly in jig — nothing forced or twisted to fit

- Consistent contact round the full joint circumference — not just touching on one side

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with spatial difficulties: instructor talks them through each joint position in the jig step by step

### Block 4 | Adjustment Round (12:45–1:15, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Re-profile marked joints. Re-check each one in the jig. Sign off each joint in workbook when passed.

- Any joint that cannot reach 1mm tolerance today must be flagged — plan extra time at start of Day 4.

### Block 5 | Workbook & Oral Q&A (2:15–3:00, 45 min)

**What To Do**

- Learners complete dry-fit inspection sheet: list each joint, gap measurement, pass/fail.

- Oral Q&A while circulating (2 questions per learner):

**Oral Q&A Questions**

- “Why is a tight joint stronger than a gappy one?”

- “What would happen if you shaped too much off a tube end?”

- “Why do we dry-fit before we glue?”

- “What does the jig do that you couldn't do without it?”

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Written Q&A option for learners who struggle with verbal responses

- Allow use of key terms card during oral Q&A
