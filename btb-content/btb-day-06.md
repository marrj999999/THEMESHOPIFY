# Day 6: De-Jig & Structural Inspection

## SESSION OVERVIEW

The epoxy has cured overnight. Today learners remove their frames from the jig for the first time and carry out a full structural inspection. This is the moment of truth. A good inspection is thorough, honest, and written down.

## Materials & Tools Checklist

#### TOOLS

- Steel rule — for checking joint alignment

- Try square — for checking tube angles

- Small torch or inspection light — for looking inside joint zones

- Magnifying glass (optional) — for close void inspection

#### MATERIALS (if remediation needed)

- Water-based epoxy — small reserve batch (kept locked)

- Mixing cups and sticks

- Fine sandpaper 80-grit — for surface prep before re-bond

#### PPE

- Safety glasses — required throughout

- Nitrile gloves — if any remediation epoxy is used

- Dust masks — not required unless sanding for remediation

## Key Terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Void | An air pocket or gap inside a cured joint. Voids are weak points where cracks can start |
| Delamination | Where two bonded surfaces have separated. If a joint delaminates it has failed |
| Sound joint | A joint that is correctly bonded, with no voids, no delamination, and correct alignment |
| Alignment | Whether the tubes are at the correct angle and position after bonding. Misalignment affects frame geometry and safety |
| Remediation | Fixing a problem that has been found. Not all joint faults need full re-bonding — some can be filled |
| Load bearing | Carrying the weight and forces during use. Every joint in this frame is load bearing — there are no cosmetic joints |

## Safety Reminders

> **SAFETY — Day 6**
> 
> Handle de-jigged frames carefully — grip the tube, not the joint. The joint is new and should not be stressed yet
> Do not load-test a joint by hand force — use visual and physical inspection methods only
> If remediation epoxy is needed: full Day 5 safety protocol applies — gloves, glasses, ventilation
> Masking tape removal — pull slowly, parallel to the joint. Sharp pulling can lift cured epoxy
> Cured epoxy dust (from any sanding for remediation) is a respiratory hazard — dust mask required

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | What Makes a Good Joint (9:30–10:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Before learners see their frames, discuss and write on the board: what does a good joint look like? feel like?

- Guide them to identify: full contact round circumference, no visible voids, no movement under gentle pressure, tubes in correct alignment, clean bond line.

- Learners write these criteria in their workbook as their inspection checklist.

**Example of What To Say**

- “You are going to inspect your own frame. Before you do, you need to know what you are looking for. Write it down first.”

- “An honest report on your own work is harder than it sounds. If you find a problem, tell me. We can almost always fix it.”

**What To Look For**

- Criteria that are vague (“looks good”) — push for specifics

- Learners who are anxious about what they will find — reassure: we can fix most things

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Pre-printed inspection criteria card available for learners who struggle to generate the list

### Block 2 | De-Jig (10:00–10:20, 20 min)

**What To Do**

- One frame de-jigged at a time. Instructor supervises.

- Learner removes clamps or bands. Lifts frame out of jig by the main tubes — not by the joints.

- Remove masking tape slowly and carefully.

- Place frame on bench. Stand back and look at it.

**Example of What To Say**

- “There is your frame. You built that. Now we check it properly.”

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Instructor de-jigs for learners with grip difficulties; learner directs each step

### Block 3 | Structural Inspection (10:20–11:30, 70 min)

**What To Do**

- Each learner works through their inspection checklist joint by joint. Instructor present throughout.

- For each joint: visual inspection (full circumference), gentle physical check (no movement), alignment check with try square.

- Record each joint in workbook: PASS or FAIL, and a written note of what was observed.

- Instructor writes their own parallel observation notes. These are the evidence record.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Report what you see, not what you hoped to see. That is what makes a good inspector.”

**What To Look For**

- Learners who only look at the “good” side of a joint and miss problems on the other side

- Learners who over-rate their joints — gentle challenge: “Let's look at this again together.”

- Any joint with movement under gentle pressure — this is a structural failure, flag immediately

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with literacy difficulties: verbal report with instructor scribing observations

- Use numbered joint diagram in workbook so learners can record by number rather than writing component names

### Block 4 | Remediation Round (11:45–12:30, 45 min)

**What To Do**

- Any joint rated FAIL: assess whether it can be repaired or needs full re-bonding.

- Minor surface void: lightly sand joint area, apply small fresh epoxy fill, re-clamp.

- Misalignment: discuss options with learner. Some misalignments can be corrected at assembly. Major ones may affect Day 9 safety test.

- Full joint failure: re-clean surfaces, re-apply epoxy, re-jig, re-clamp. Note on inspection record.

**What To Look For**

- Learners upset by a failed joint — reframe: “Finding this now means we can fix it. Finding it after a child rides the bike is not an option.”

### Block 5 | Workbook Completion & Oral Q&A (1:30–2:30, 60 min)

**Oral Q&A Questions**

- “What is a void, and why is it a problem?”

- “How did you check alignment on your frame?”

- “If you found a failed joint at this stage, what would you do?”

- “Why is it important to do the inspection yourself, not just rely on the instructor?”
