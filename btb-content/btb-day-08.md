# Day 8: Painting & Finishing

## SESSION OVERVIEW

Today the frame gets its colour. Learners choose their finish, apply primer and topcoat, and finish with a protective lacquer. Water-based products only. The bike is starting to look like something someone will be proud to give.

## Materials & Tools Checklist

#### FINISHING MATERIALS — WATER-BASED ONLY

- Water-based primer — 1 tin shared (white or grey)

- Water-based topcoat paint — colour selection available (confirm range with security)

- Water-based clear lacquer (satin or matt) — 1 tin shared

- Disposable foam brushes — minimum 3 per learner (primer, colour, lacquer)

- Mixing sticks

- Clean water for brush rinsing

- Waste water container for rinsing

#### TOOLS AND EQUIPMENT

- Hanging rail or drying rack for frames

- Old newspaper or drop cloth for bench protection

- Scissors for cutting brush ends if needed

#### PPE

- Nitrile gloves — required for all painting

- Safety glasses — required throughout

- Dust mask FFP1 — not required for water-based, but available

- Old apron or overalls — water-based paint washes out but still stains

## Key Terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Primer | The first coat. It seals the surface and helps the topcoat stick. Without primer, paint can peel |
| Topcoat | The colour coat. Usually applied in two thin coats rather than one thick one |
| Lacquer | A clear protective coat over the topcoat. It makes the finish tougher and easier to clean |
| Run | A drip or sag in a paint coat caused by too much paint applied in one place |
| Tack | Slightly sticky — the stage between wet and dry. When a coat is “tack-dry” it is ready for the next coat |
| Coverage | How well the paint hides the surface underneath. Full coverage means no bare patches or see-through areas |

## Safety Reminders

> **SAFETY — Day 8**
> 
> Water-based finishes ONLY — check every product label before opening. No solvent-based products permitted
> Gloves on before handling any paint or lacquer
> Rinse water must be collected and disposed of as waste, not poured down the sink in large quantities
> Paint containers must be resealed and stored after each use — do not leave open
> Wet paint on the floor is a slip hazard — clean up spills immediately
> Do not touch wet frames — fingerprints in fresh paint ruin the finish and require re-sanding

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | Paint Briefing & Colour Choice (9:30–10:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Lay out all colour options. Let learners handle the tins and look at colour charts.

- Guide colour choice with a question: “What colour would your child like? Or what colour would make them happy when they see it?”

- Explain the coating sequence: primer first, then two colour coats, then lacquer. No shortcuts.

- Demo brush technique on a scrap piece: thin coat, work in one direction, do not go back over drying paint.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Thin coats are better than thick coats. A thick coat runs. Two thin coats give you a better finish than one thick one.”

- “This is going to be a gift. The person who gets this will see the care you put into the finish.”

**What To Look For**

- Learners who are excited about the colour choice — good. Channel that into careful application

- Learners who are indifferent — gently connect the colour choice to the child who will ride it

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with fine motor difficulties: wider brush, frame held in vice for application

- Visual impairment: choose high-contrast colour for easier application visibility

### Block 2 | Primer Coat (10:00–10:45, 45 min)

**What To Do**

- Apply primer to the whole frame. Thin, even coat. Work section by section.

- Technique: load the brush lightly (not dripping), apply in long strokes along the tube, feather the ends.

- Pay attention to joints: these are harder to coat evenly — use a smaller brush or a corner of the main brush.

- Instructor checks each frame: no runs, no bare patches, no pooling at joints.

- Hang or prop frames to dry — do not lay flat.

**What To Look For**

- Runs at joint zones — common because the surface is irregular there. Work slower, less paint

- Bare patches on the underside of tubes — learners often miss the back

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Allow learner to rotate frame on a stand rather than holding it throughout

### Block 3 | Topcoat 1 (10:45–11:15, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Check primer is tack-dry before starting. Touch gently with fingertip — no paint comes off, slightly tacky.

- Apply first colour coat. Same technique as primer. This is the base layer — full coverage is not expected yet.

- Break timed to allow drying.

### Block 4 | Topcoat 2 (11:45–12:30, 45 min)

**What To Do**

- Topcoat 1 should now be tack-dry. Check before proceeding.

- Apply second colour coat. This is the finishing coat — full, even coverage expected. No bare patches.

- Instructor spot check on every frame before lunch. Address any issues now.

- Frame to drying rack or hanging point. Do not lay flat. Do not touch during lunch.

**What To Look For**

- Runs from applying topcoat 2 over topcoat 1 before fully tack-dry

- Missed sections on bottom of tubes or inside curves

### Block 5 | Lacquer Coat (1:30–2:15, 45 min)

**What To Do**

- Confirm topcoat 2 is dry (not just tack-dry — fully dry for lacquer). Water-based topcoat should be dry after 60 minutes.

- Apply clear lacquer over entire frame. Thin, even coat. Same technique as topcoat.

- Instructor final check on each frame: full lacquer coverage, no runs.

- Hang to dry overnight. Do not touch until Day 9.

**Example of What To Say**

- “The lacquer is the coat that protects everything else. It needs to go on evenly and left to harden properly. Tomorrow we assemble. Tonight we wait.”

### Block 6 | Finish Inspection & Workbook (2:15–2:45, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Learners complete finish record in workbook: all coats applied, product names, any issues, instructor sign-off.

- Oral Q&A (2 per learner).

**Oral Q&A Questions**

- “Why is primer applied before the colour coat?”

- “What does the lacquer do that the paint alone doesn't?”

- “Why do we use two thin colour coats instead of one thick one?”

## Differentiation

#### FOR LOW LITERACY

The workbook entry can be entirely oral — "What did you pick and why?" Scribe verbatim. The reasoning does not need to be complex: "I picked blue because that's what he likes" is complete evidence.

#### FOR EMOTIONAL RESPONSES TO PERSONALISATION

Some learners will find choosing a colour for their child's bike emotionally significant. Keep it grounded in the practical: the choice is about the bike. Do not extend or probe emotional responses. Let the choice happen and move on to the painting.

#### FOR ADHD

One tube at a time. The drying wait between coats is the hardest part for ADHD learners — use break time productively (workbook, clean brushes, check decals). Do not leave learners idle during drying time.
