# Day 10: Quality Review & Celebration

## SESSION OVERVIEW

Today is the finish line. Learners complete any outstanding work, do a final quality review of their bike, complete their workbook, and have their achievement recognised. This session carries real weight. Treat it that way.

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | Outstanding Day 9 Items (9:30–10:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Check Day 9 notes. Any outstanding fail items on any bike: address these before anything else.

- Do not move to quality review until all outstanding items are resolved.

- Instructor signs off resolved items on the existing Day 9 checklist with date and note.

### Block 2 | Final Quality Review (10:00–11:00, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- Each learner works through the 8-item quality review on their own bike.

- This is their review — instructor observes and records alongside.

- Encourage learners to be honest about item 8: “Would you be proud to give this to a child?” This is not a trick question. It is a genuine quality check.

- Instructor final sign-off on quality review sheet.

**Example of What To Say**

- “You built this. You are the best judge of whether it is good. Look at it honestly. Tell me what you see.”

- “The fact that you can find things to improve means you have learned to see quality. That is the most important thing you are taking away today.”

**What To Look For**

- Learners who are too self-critical — help them see what they have achieved

- Learners who are dismissive — gently challenge them to look more carefully

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Allow verbal quality review with instructor scribing for learners with literacy difficulties

- If a learner is emotional during the review, give them space and return when ready

### Block 3 | Workbook Completion & Portfolio (11:15–12:30, 75 min)

**What To Do**

- Work through each learner's workbook. Check every page. Address any gaps now.

- Ensure all assessment criteria have evidence recorded. Gaps can be addressed verbally — record the verbal response and note it as such.

- Learner and instructor both sign and date the portfolio completion page.

**What To Look For**

- Assessment criteria with no evidence at all — can often be covered by a short verbal Q&A recorded now

- Incomplete dates or signatures on earlier pages

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- For learners with significant literacy barriers: ensure verbal evidence is recorded throughout workbook with instructor notes, not blank pages

### Block 4 | Final Oral Assessment (1:30–2:30, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- [ ] Formal oral assessment — each learner is asked 4–5 questions about their own bike and the work they did over 10 days.

- [ ] Questions cover all 8 LOs. Record responses in observation notes.

- [ ] This is a conversation, not an exam. Approach it as a review of what they have built and learned.

**Final Oral Assessment Questions (choose 4–5 per learner)**

- [ ] “Tell me about the child you built this for. Why did you choose those measurements?”

- [ ] “How did you select your bamboo tubes, and what would you reject?”

- [ ] “What was the hardest joint to get right, and what did you do to fix it?”

- [ ] “What did you do on Day 5 to make sure the epoxy bonds would hold?”

- [ ] “If you found a failed joint today, what would you do?”

- [ ] “Why does the surface prep matter even though it's hidden under the paint?”

- [ ] “What does BS EN 14765 mean, and why did we use it?”

- [ ] “What would you do differently if you built another one?”

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- [ ] Allow learner to handle their bike during oral assessment as a prompt

- [ ] Reframe questions in simpler language if needed — the standard is understanding, not vocabulary

### Block 5 | Celebration & Close (2:30–3:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Acknowledge what has been built. Read out (briefly) each learner's name and what they made. Not a speech — a moment.

- If certificates are available: present them. If not: confirm when they will be issued.

- Ask each learner: “What are you most proud of?” Record their answers — these can go into the portfolio as a final reflection.

- Discuss the handover process: how the bike will get to the child, what happens next.

- Final tool count. Collect workbooks. Officer sign-off. Workshop secure.

**Example of What To Say**

- “You came in on Day 1 with a bamboo tube and an idea. You are leaving with a safe, finished bike for a child. That is what this course is.”

- “The qualification says you know how to build. The bike says you actually did it. Both of those things are yours now.”

## Differentiation

#### FOR LOW LITERACY — PORTFOLIO

A portfolio where most written entries are scribed by the instructor is entirely valid. What matters is that the learner's voice is there — their words, their signature, your annotation noting "recorded verbally, scribed by instructor." The OCN standard is about demonstrated competence, not writing ability.

#### FOR EMOTIONAL RESPONSES

Day 10 may surface a range of emotional responses — pride, grief, frustration, sadness. Do not manage these away. Give space, keep the practical moving, and let the session end with the work: "You built this. That is done." If a learner needs support beyond what you can provide, ensure they are connected to the prison pastoral team before they leave the workshop today.

#### FOR RESISTANT OR DISENGAGED LEARNERS

Some learners will remain outwardly disengaged even on the final day. Accept it. If they completed the work, they get the certificate. The qualification is earned by the building, not by the emotional performance of caring about it.

## Instructor Notes

#### CERTIFICATES — HANDLE WITH CARE

Certificates must be obtained from the OCN centre before Day 10. Confirm with your programme coordinator at least one week in advance. If certificates have not arrived, run all other elements of Day 10 and issue certificates as soon as they arrive — do not delay the programme sign-off for a printing delay.

#### PORTFOLIO TRANSFER

Portfolios are not given directly to learners in the workshop — they are filed by the instructor and transferred through official channels. In most establishments, this means handing to the wing officer for filing, or retaining until release. Confirm the correct process with prison administration before Day 10.

#### BIKES — TRANSFER TO FAMILY LIAISON

Bikes are transferred to the family liaison officer, not to learners. This is the standard process — the bike is a gift to a child, not personal property to be held by the learner. Confirm this process clearly with learners before Day 10 to avoid misunderstandings on the day.

#### POST-PROGRAMME REPORTING

After Day 10, complete your programme report: cohort size, completion rate, any learner pastoral concerns, assessment outcomes, any issues with materials or facilities. File with your programme coordinator within 5 working days.
