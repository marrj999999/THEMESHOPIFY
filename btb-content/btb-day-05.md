# Day 5: Epoxy Bonding

## SESSION OVERVIEW

**CRITICAL DAY. Epoxy in use. This is the point of no return.** Every joint is bonded today and left to cure overnight. Mistakes here are permanent. The session runs to a strict sequence. Learners who are not focused do not apply epoxy — the instructor steps in.

## Materials & Tools Checklist

#### CHEMICALS — INSTRUCTOR CONTROLLED ONLY

- Water-based structural epoxy resin — sufficient for 5 frames

- Epoxy hardener — matched to resin, correct ratio confirmed

- Mixing cups (wax-free) — minimum 10

- Wooden mixing sticks — minimum 20

- Digital scales accurate to 0.1g — 1 per mixing station

- Cleaning solvent (water-based, approved) — small bottle, locked

- Damp cloths for spill control — pre-prepared

#### TOOLS AND EQUIPMENT

- Frame jigs — 1 per learner

- Clamps or rubber bands for joint support during cure

- Application brushes (disposable) — minimum 10

- Timer (for pot life tracking)

#### PPE — NON-NEGOTIABLE TODAY

- Nitrile gloves — double-gloved during application

- Safety glasses — on before any epoxy container is opened

- Chemical apron — 1 per active learner, instructor included

- Dust mask not required but ventilation is mandatory

## Key Terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Structural adhesive | A glue strong enough to hold parts of a load-bearing structure together. Epoxy is a structural adhesive |
| Pot life | The time after mixing when epoxy can still be used. After pot life ends, the epoxy starts to set and cannot be spread |
| Exotherm | Heat produced when epoxy cures. A hot pot means the epoxy is curing fast — you have less time |
| Adhesion | How well two surfaces stick together. Good adhesion requires clean, dry, key surfaces and the right amount of glue |
| Cure time | How long until the epoxy is fully hard and the joint can be loaded. 24 hours minimum for this product |
| Bond line | The thin layer of epoxy between the two surfaces. Too thick a bond line is weaker, not stronger |

## Safety Reminders

> **SAFETY — Day 5 CRITICAL RULES**
> 
> Double gloves on before any epoxy is handled — chemical apron on
> Safety glasses on before any container is opened — no exceptions
> Ventilation must be confirmed working before learners enter
> One learner bonds at a time unless two instructors are present
> Pot life is the clock — start the timer the moment mixing begins
> Skin contact: stop immediately, wash with soap and water for 10 minutes, report to instructor
> Eye contact: eyewash station, 15 minutes flushing, send to healthcare
> No learner works alone with epoxy — instructor must be within arm's reach throughout
> No bonded frame is moved or touched for 24 hours — sign the jig with the time bonded
> Water-based epoxy ONLY — confirm product before session opens

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | Learner Entry & Safety Check (9:30–9:50, 20 min)

**What To Do**

- Meet learners at the workshop door. PPE on before entry.

- Verbal safety check: ask each learner in turn: “What is the mix ratio?” and “What do you do if epoxy touches your skin?”

- Any learner who cannot answer does not mix today. They observe and record instead. Do not make this a punishment — frame it as a safety decision.

- Brief the sequence for the day: who bonds when, what everyone else does while waiting.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Today is the most important practical day of the course. We work slowly and carefully. If at any point you are unsure, you stop and you ask. There is no rushing.”

- “The bond you make today is what keeps that child safe when they ride this bike. Take that seriously.”

**What To Look For**

- Any learner who seems anxious, agitated, or distracted — take them aside before bonding starts

- PPE worn correctly — check gloves are fully on, glasses seated, apron tied

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Anxiety around chemicals: allow pre-session walkthrough of the workspace before others arrive

- Learner who cannot pass verbal safety check: they observe and scribe — evidence collected via workbook and observation

### Block 2 | Bonding Sequence — One Learner at a Time

**Repeat this block for each learner:**

**What To Do**

- Learner retrieves their frame from the jig (instructor checks frame is correct).

- Learner measures out resin and hardener on scales. Instructor confirms weights before mixing begins.

- Learner mixes for 3 full minutes. Start timer. Check consistency — no streaks visible.

- Learner applies epoxy to first joint using brush. Work quickly but not rushed. Fill joint completely but no excess.

- Joint placed back in jig. Clamp or rubber band applied.

- Repeat for each joint. Do not exceed pot life — mix a fresh batch for each joint if needed.

- Instructor records observation notes in real time: mix ratio correct, application method, joint positioned correctly.

- Learner writes time bonded on masking tape of each joint.

**Example of What To Say (during bonding)**

- “Check the scales before you mix. Once you mix it, you use it.”

- “Not too much. The bond line should be thin and even, not a blob.”

- “In the jig. Good. Now clamp. Now we wait.”

**What To Look For**

- Incorrect mix ratio — stop immediately, discard, start fresh

- Joints placed back in jig out of position — correct before epoxy starts to set

- Epoxy on skin — stop work, wash immediately

- Exotherm in mixing cup — hot cup means epoxy is curing fast, use it or discard

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with fine motor difficulties: instructor applies epoxy, learner directs and signs off each step

- Learners with anxiety: work in a quiet corner if possible, one-to-one ratio

- Memory difficulties: step-by-step laminated card at the mixing station

### Block 3 | Clamp Check & Jig Sign-Off (12:25–12:55)

**What To Do**

- Walk round all frames in jigs. Check: all joints in correct position, clamps secure, no epoxy drips on critical surfaces.

- Write on each jig: learner initials, date bonded, time bonded, time when cure is complete (24 hours later).

- Instructor signs off each frame. Tape a “DO NOT TOUCH — CURING” notice to each jig.

**Example of What To Say**

- “This frame is now curing. It will be ready tomorrow. If anyone moves it before then, the joint fails. Nobody touches these frames tonight.”

### Block 4 | Clean-Up (12:55–1:30)

**What To Do**

- All mixing equipment cleaned immediately with the approved cleaner.

- Any unused mixed epoxy: leave in cup to cure fully (1 hour minimum), then dispose as solid waste per COSHH.

- Gloves removed carefully — turn inside out, dispose into sealed bag.

- Aprons wiped down with damp cloth. Check for epoxy contamination.

- All learners wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before lunch — mandatory, even with glove use.

- Epoxy containers and scales returned to secure store. Signed back in.

### Block 5 | Workbook Bonding Log & Q&A (2:30–3:00)

**What To Do**

- Learners complete bonding log: joints bonded, epoxy batch used, time bonded, any issues noted.

- Oral Q&A (2 per learner): “Why did you weigh the components before mixing?” “What would happen if you moved the frame in the first hour?”

- Preview Day 6: “Tomorrow the frames are ready. We inspect every joint. We are looking for strength, not beauty.”

## Differentiation

#### FOR ALL LEARNERS — STAGGER IS ESSENTIAL

Solo instructor + 5 learners doing epoxy = highest-risk supervision scenario of the programme. The stagger protocol (2-2-1) is not optional. Do not allow more than 2 learners to be actively mixing simultaneously.

#### FOR ANXIOUS LEARNERS

"Together" supervision reduces anxiety and produces better joints. Stand with the learner for their first joint — coach them through it physically, not just verbally.

#### FOR EMOTIONAL REACTIONS

This is often the day when it becomes real for learners — the frame feels like a thing now, not just components. Keep the energy practical. "Good joint. Next one." The work moves faster than any emotional processing needs to.
