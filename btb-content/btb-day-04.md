# Day 4: Frame Refinement & Bonding Prep

## Key Terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Epoxy | A two-part adhesive. Mix the resin and hardener together and it sets hard. Once mixed, it cannot be unmixed |
| Mix ratio | The exact amount of resin compared to hardener. Getting this wrong means the epoxy will not cure properly |
| Pot life | How long you have to use the epoxy after mixing before it starts to go hard in the pot. Usually 30–45 minutes |
| Cure | The process of epoxy going from liquid to solid. Full cure takes 24 hours at room temperature |
| COSHH | Control of Substances Hazardous to Health — the rules about how to handle chemicals safely |
| Masking | Putting tape over areas you do not want to get glue on. Keeps bond lines clean and tidy |
| Pre-bond inspection | The final check that everything is ready before glue goes on. Once bonded, changes are very difficult |

## Safety Reminders

> **SAFETY — Day 4 Non-Negotiables**
> 
> Epoxy is handled by instructor ONLY today — learners observe only
> Nitrile gloves must be worn by anyone near the epoxy demo
> Uncured epoxy is a skin sensitiser — contact causes allergic reactions that get worse over time
> COSHH sheet must be read and signed by all learners before epoxy use tomorrow
> No eating or drinking in the workshop — especially important on chemical prep days
> Spills are cleaned up immediately with damp cloth — not dry cloth (which spreads contamination)
> Demo waste (mixed epoxy in cup) must be allowed to cure fully before disposal — do not pour liquid epoxy down the drain

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | Carry-Over Joint Work (9:30–10:30, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- Retrieve Day 3 instructor notes. Identify any learner whose joints need further work.

- [ ] Prioritise those learners. Others can review workbook or start masking tape prep.

- [ ] Each refined joint must be re-checked in the jig. Instructor signs off when passed.

- [ ] Do not rush. A joint that fails tomorrow cannot be easily fixed.

**Example of What To Say**

- [ ] “Today is your last chance. After tomorrow the glue decides. Make sure you're happy with every joint before you tape it up.”

**What To Look For**

- [ ] Learners who rush refinement to reach the masking stage — quality gates must hold

- [ ] Any new splits or damage from storage — inspect tube condition before starting

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- [ ] Allow learner to describe what a good joint should feel like before checking their own

- [ ] Peer-assisted checking for learners who struggle to self-assess

### Block 2 | Masking Tape Application (10:30–11:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- [ ] Demo: apply masking tape 30mm back from each joint opening on both tubes. Run the tape neatly with no bubbles or overlaps.

- [ ] Each learner masks their own frame in the jig.

- [ ] Check: no tape inside the joint zone (it will block the bond). Tape should go right to the edge of the joint zone and stop.

**Example of What To Say**

- “The tape is not just for tidiness. It stops epoxy running down the tube and creating a weak spot or a stress riser. Clean tape lines = clean joints.”

**What To Look For**

- Tape covering part of the joint face — this will block adhesion

- Tape applied while frame is out of jig and then replaced wrong — mask it in situ

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Fine motor: assist with tape application, learner directs placement

- Use wider tape if fine placement is too difficult

### Block 3 | Epoxy Safety Briefing & Workbook (11:15–12:00, 45 min)

**What To Do**

- Distribute COSHH sheet. Read through key hazards together — skin sensitisation, eye irritation, ventilation requirement.

- Explain mix ratio for the specific product being used tomorrow. Write the ratio on the board.

- Learners complete workbook epoxy safety section: list 3 hazards, 3 precautions, what to do if skin contact occurs.

- Each learner signs the COSHH acknowledgement sheet — this is a legal requirement before chemical use.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Epoxy does not wash off easily. It cures on your skin. Gloves are not optional — they're the rule.”

- “If you get epoxy on your skin, tell me immediately. We wash it off straight away with soap and water. Do not leave it.”

**What To Look For**

- Learners who sign without reading — ask them to tell you one hazard before signing

- Any learner who mentions a skin allergy — flag to health lead before Day 5

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Low literacy: read COSHH sheet aloud together, learner confirms verbally then instructor scribes

- Large-print COSHH sheet available

### Block 4 | Epoxy Test Mix Demo (12:00–12:30, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Instructor only handles epoxy. Learners stand back 1 metre minimum and observe.

- Demonstrate: measure out correct ratio by weight (scales), mix thoroughly for 3 minutes, check consistency (no streaks), note start time for pot life.

- Show correct application technique — small amount, work into joint surface, avoid air bubbles.

- Allow mixed batch to cure in the pot. Show learners what cured epoxy looks like tomorrow morning.

- Learners record steps and timings in workbook observation notes.

**What To Look For**

- Learners recording the mix ratio — they must know this for tomorrow

- Questions about pot life and what happens if they go over time — good engagement

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with visual impairment: closer observation position is acceptable for demo (still no contact)

- Provide step-by-step reference card for the mixing process

### Block 5 | Pre-Bond Inspection (1:30–2:30, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- Sit with each learner and their framed-up, masked frame. Work through the pre-bond inspection checklist together.

- Check: all joints pass gap test, masking tape applied correctly, tubes correctly labelled, frame geometry in jig correct.

- Sign and date the inspection record for each frame.

- Do not sign off any frame that does not meet standard. Flag issues and agree a plan.

**Example of What To Say**

- “I am signing this because I believe it is ready. You are the builder. Do YOU believe it is ready?”

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Use the pre-bond checklist as a visual guide — learner ticks each item as it is checked

### Block 6 | Day Close & Day 5 Briefing (2:30–3:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- Brief the whole group on exactly what will happen tomorrow. Walk through the bonding sequence from start to end.

- Emphasise: tomorrow is focused work. No rushing, no distractions, no skipping steps.

- Collect and secure workbooks. Lock away epoxy and chemical materials.

- Full tool count. Shadow board. Officer sign-off.

**What To Say**

- “Tomorrow the bike becomes permanent. Everything you've done this week either holds together or it doesn't. You're ready. Come in focused.”

## Differentiation

#### FOR ANXIOUS LEARNERS (epoxy briefing)

Frame the briefing as professional practice, not danger management: "Every tradesperson who uses adhesives goes through this. You're learning how it's done properly." The rules are simple and the risks are manageable with correct PPE.

#### FOR LOW LITERACY

Read the epoxy briefing sheet aloud completely. Ask oral confirmation of key rules before signing. The mixing ratio card must be visual. Workbook entry can be two bullet points or verbally dictated.

#### FOR ADHD

The practice mixing session is active and concrete — good for ADHD learners. The 2-minute mixing timer provides structure. Ensure PPE is on before dispensing begins — a quick visual checklist before each learner starts.
