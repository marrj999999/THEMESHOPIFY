# Day 2: Bamboo Selection & Preparation

## SESSION OVERVIEW

Today learners go into the bamboo stock and select every tube they will use in their frame. They cut to length using their Day 1 specification. Getting this right matters — a tube cut too short cannot be uncut.

## Materials & Tools Checklist

#### MATERIALS

- Pencil for mark-out (NOT permanent marker on cut lines)

#### TOOLS (all on shadow board, signed in/out)

- Mitre box and saw — 1 per 2 learners maximum

- Steel rule — 1 per learner

- Marking gauge (or tape measure and pencil) — 1 per learner

- Callipers for diameter check — 1 per bench

- Try square — 1 per bench

#### PPE

- Safety glasses — required for all cutting

- Dust masks FFP1 — required when cutting bamboo

- Nitrile gloves — optional for handling stock

## Key Terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Node | The solid ring inside a bamboo tube. It makes the tube stronger but affects where you can cut and shape |
| Wall thickness | How thick the bamboo walls are. Thicker walls mean more strength but more weight |
| Split | A crack running along the bamboo. A split tube is too weak to use in a frame |
| Mark-out | Drawing the cut line on a tube before you cut. Marking out carefully prevents mistakes |
| Mitre box | A guide that holds the saw at a fixed angle so your cuts are straight and square |
| Compression | A squeezing force. Bamboo handles compression well — it is very strong when pushed end-to-end |
| Tension | A pulling or stretching force. Bamboo also handles tension well because of the long fibres running along it |

## Safety Reminders

> **SAFETY — Day 2 Non-Negotiables**
> 
> Dust masks MUST be worn for all bamboo cutting — bamboo dust is a respiratory hazard
> Safety glasses on before any saw is picked up
> One person cutting at a time — no exceptions
> Saw teeth are sharp — pass the saw handle-first, never tooth-first
> No cutting without instructor check of mark-out first
> Bamboo splits can splinter — reject any cracked tube immediately, do not attempt to cut it
> Full tool count before lunch AND at end of day

## TEACHING SEQUENCE

### Block 1 | Recap & Bamboo Quality Briefing (9:30–10:00, 30 min)

**What You'll Need**

- [ ] Workbooks — specification table page open

- [ ] Good and bad bamboo samples (pre-selected for demonstration)

**What To Do**

- [ ] Ask learners to read out their bamboo specifications from Day 1. Check all are complete.

- [ ] Show the good/bad bamboo samples. Ask: “What's wrong with this one?”

- Cover 4 rejection criteria: splits, soft or rotted sections, inadequate wall thickness, bad node position.

- Explain node rule: “Nodes must not fall inside a joint zone. A node where the epoxy goes will stop the adhesive bonding properly.”

**Example of What To Say**

- “A weak tube on a child's bike is not just a quality problem. It is a safety problem. We reject anything we are not sure about.”

- “Bamboo grows in a factory called the ground. Every piece is different. Your job is to choose the right one.”

**What To Look For**

- Learners who skip the quality check and go straight for the nearest tube

- Good justifications for tube choices — note as AC 2.2 evidence

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Visual reference card with photos (drawings) of good vs rejected bamboo for learners with memory difficulties

- Pair weaker readers with a peer for the quality briefing

### Block 2 | Bamboo Selection (10:00–11:00, 60 min)

**What You'll Need**

- Bamboo stock

- Callipers for diameter checks

- Workbook bamboo selection record page

**What To Do**

- Take learners to bamboo stock one or two at a time. Others continue workbook from Day 1 or study key terms.

- Each learner selects all tubes for their frame and records: component, diameter measured, length needed, reason accepted or rejected.

- Instructor countersigns each selection record.

**Example of What To Say**

- “If you wouldn't let your child ride it, don't build it with that tube.”

- “Take your time. You can't uncut a tube.”

**What To Look For**

- Diameter measurements checked with callipers, not guessed

- Selection record filled in during selection, not after

- Any tube with visible splits or soft spots — reject immediately

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with fine motor difficulties: allow instructor to hold tube while learner operates callipers

- Pre-selected tube sets available as backup if a learner cannot manage stock independently

### Block 3 | Mark-Out & Instructor Check (11:15–12:00, 45 min)

**What To Do**

- Learners lay out all selected tubes in order. Using specification table, they measure and mark each cut line in pencil.

- Mark clearly around the full circumference of the tube so the saw line is visible from all angles.

- Instructor checks every mark before any cutting begins. Correct errors now.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Mark all the way round the tube. If you only mark one side, the saw drifts on the other side and your cut goes wrong.”

**What To Look For**

- Marks on one side only — insist on full circumference marking

- Measurement errors — check against specification before any cutting

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Pre-printed measurement templates for learners with dyscalculia

- Use coloured tape for mark lines if pencil is hard to see

### Block 4 | Cutting to Length (12:00–12:30, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- One learner cuts at a time. Others observe or continue workbook.

- Demonstrate saw technique: light pressure, let the saw do the work, keep square to the line.

- After each cut, check length against specification. Record any variance in workbook.

- Label cut tubes immediately with masking tape: learner initials + component name.

**Example of What To Say**

- “Light pressure. You're not forcing it through — you're guiding it. The teeth do the work.”

**What To Look For**

- Saw drift away from cut line — pause and correct before the cut goes too far

- Bamboo splitting at cut end — slow the stroke and reduce pressure

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Learners with limited hand strength: bench vice to hold tube, two-handed saw grip

- Additional demonstration cut by instructor before each learner's first cut

### Block 5 | Node Check & Tube Inspection (1:30–2:30, 60 min)

**What To Do**

- Lay out all cut tubes. Check node positions against joint zones marked on specification.

- Any tube with a node inside a joint zone must be re-cut (if possible) or replaced.

- Check cut ends are square. Use try square — if out of square by more than 2mm, re-cut.

- Learners record final tube lengths and node positions in workbook.

**What To Look For**

- Nodes inside joint zones — this is a structural problem, not cosmetic

- Out-of-square cut ends — will cause joint misalignment on Day 3

**SEND Adaptation Tips**

- Use a simple go/no-go jig for node position check if learner finds the measurement difficult

### Block 6 | Label, Store & Day Close (2:30–3:00, 30 min)

**What To Do**

- All tubes labelled: learner initials, component name, date cut.

- Store each learner's tube set separately and securely.

- Ask each learner: “Name one thing you checked before you cut.” Record responses.

- Full tool count. Shadow board complete. Saw count especially important. Officer sign-off.
