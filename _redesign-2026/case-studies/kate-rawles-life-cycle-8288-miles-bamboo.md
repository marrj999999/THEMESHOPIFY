# Kate Rawles built her own bamboo bike — then rode it 8,288 miles down the Andes

Kate Rawles walked into the Bamboo Bicycle Club workshop having never built a bike. She walked out with one she named Woody — and went on to ride it 8,288 miles down the spine of the Andes, from the Caribbean coast of Colombia to Patagonia.

## The challenge

Kate is an environmental writer and adventurer, and she wanted her next expedition to match its message: a long, low-carbon journey the length of South America to look at biodiversity loss — one of the most important and least-reported environmental crises of our time. That meant starting not with a bike off a shop floor, but with a pile of bamboo canes in a workshop.

## What we did

Kate came to the Bamboo Bicycle Club in London and built her own frame from scratch over a five-day course — the first bike she had ever made. The bamboo was grown at the Eden Project in Cornwall, and the joints were wrapped in Yorkshire hemp soaked in a UK-made plant-based resin. As far as we know, that makes Woody the UK's first home-grown bamboo bicycle. She named the finished bike Woody.

## The outcome

Kate rode Woody 8,288 miles — around 13,000 kilometres — from Cartagena down the spine of the Andes to Ushuaia in Patagonia, through six South American countries, reaching the bottom of the continent on 16 February 2018 after thirteen months of riding through heat, cold, dust and altitude. Along the way she met communities regenerating habitats and witnessed the damage of gold mining and oil drilling. The frame came through it all with virtually no mechanical issues. The journey became her book, *The Life Cycle: 8,000 Miles in the Andes by Bamboo Bike*, published by Icon Books in June 2023 and endorsed by the former BBC News Science Editor David Shukman, Eden Project co-founder Sir Tim Smit OBE, and Jonathon Porritt.

## In her words

> "The final distance of my ride was 8,288 miles, and Woody made it with virtually no mechanical issues at all."
> — Kate Rawles, *Swoop Patagonia*

## Why it matters

Kate's ride is the plainest test of bamboo we have. A first-time builder made a frame by hand and trusted it the full length of a continent — and it held. It says what we believe: bamboo isn't a novelty, it's a genuinely capable material, and the people who build with us can make something they'll trust with a serious journey.
