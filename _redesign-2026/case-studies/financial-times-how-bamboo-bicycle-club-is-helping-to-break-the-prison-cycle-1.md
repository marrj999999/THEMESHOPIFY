# In the Financial Times: how bamboo bikes help break the prison cycle

In September 2025 the Financial Times published a feature on the Bamboo Bicycle Club's prison work — "How Bamboo Bicycle Club is helping to break the prison cycle", by Marion Willingham. It put our Build to Bond programme in front of a national readership.

## What the FT covered

The piece focused on Build to Bond at HMP Lowdham Grange, a Category-B men's prison in Nottinghamshire. We run an on-site workshop of around 2,000 sq ft, where Makers take a six-week course, roughly four hours a day. They move from workshop safety and material science through to building a bamboo frame, mechanics and finishing — earning OCN Level 1 and 2 accreditation in sustainable manufacturing along the way.

The FT picked up on what it called the "IKEA effect": the extra affection people reserve for things they have built with their own hands. In Build to Bond, that object is a bike a father builds for his child.

## Why it matters

The article set the work in its evidence base. Ministry of Justice research finds that people in prison who keep up family contact are 39% less likely to reoffend. Build to Bond gives fathers a way to make something meaningful for their children — a tangible reconnection, not just a certificate. (That 39% is research context, not a claim about our own results.)

## In their words

> "Innovative projects, such as Build to Bond, support rehabilitation and help people leave prison as better citizens, boosting the economy and keeping our streets safe."
>
> — James Timpson (Lord Timpson), UK Prisons Minister

> "I don't want everyone to go out and be a bike builder. I want them to be like, 'I'm really interested in the engineering of this, I'm going to do an engineering course,' or 'I'm really interested in the look of this, I'm going to do a graphic design course.'"
>
> — James Marr, founder, Bamboo Bicycle Club
