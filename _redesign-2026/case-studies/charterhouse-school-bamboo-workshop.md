# The teacher who spent years trying to get bamboo bikes into a school

Andy Thomson had tried more than once to get bamboo bike building onto the timetable. When he moved to Charterhouse School in Godalming, Surrey, after two decades teaching elsewhere, he finally had the space and the appetite to make it happen.

## The challenge

Andy Thomson is a Director of Design Technology and Engineering. He had wanted to run a real bamboo bike build with students for years, but never had the room or the backing to do it. Arriving at Charterhouse, a school with a well-equipped DT department, a laser cutter and workshop space, he saw a chance to start, properly this time, with his own Year 12 pupils building complete bikes rather than just studying them.

## What we did

Rather than buy a class set on day one, Andy asked us for a single complete bamboo gravel kit, with the pre-made lugs, to trial first. The idea was simple: build one bike himself with his technician, learn the process, work out the practical questions, then roll it out to students.

In autumn 2023 Charterhouse ordered that first complete gravel setup, along with the tools and a build stand. We shipped the kit, shared the design files, and James offered to help with the build and review it with the department afterwards. In 2024 James went to the school in person to help them get the bike together, working alongside Andy and the department's technician, Mike Mellor.

## The outcome

By spring 2024 the department had the trial bike in build, with James's visit behind them and the bike close to finished. The plan from there was for Year 12 students to each build their own bamboo gravel frame, with the school reusing the aluminium lugs year on year so the cost per pupil could come down over time.

## In their words

> "Thanks again for coming to visit. You have given us extra mojo to get our bike made."
>
> — Andy Thomson, Director of Design Technology and Engineering, Charterhouse School

## Why it matters

A bamboo frame built in a school workshop is a real thing a young person makes with their own hands and can ride away on, the opposite of a worksheet. Helping a department like Charterhouse's get the first one built, on their own site with their own staff, is how the craft takes root and reaches the next group of pupils. Across fourteen years we've taught more than 4,000 people to build, in over 36 countries, since 2012.
