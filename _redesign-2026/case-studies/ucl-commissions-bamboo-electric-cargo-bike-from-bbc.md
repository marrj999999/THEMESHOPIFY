# UCL Master's Engineers Build a 150kg Bamboo Cargo Bike

When you ask trained engineers to put bamboo under load, you stop guessing and start measuring.
That is exactly what happened when Bamboo Bicycle Club ran a live design brief with Master's
students at University College London.

## The brief

Over a six-month project, UCL Master's students worked with Bamboo Bicycle Club to test the
structural properties of bamboo and design a working cargo bike — one built to carry a **150kg
load** for a low-resource farming context. Rather than treating bamboo as a curiosity, the brief
asked students to approach it as a serious structural material and back every decision with data.

## What we did

Bamboo Bicycle Club brought its frame-building craft — bamboo tube preparation and natural-fibre
composite joinery — and the students brought formal engineering methodology. Together they tested
how bamboo carries load, examined joint behaviour, and developed a cargo-bike design sized for a
real-world carrying job rather than a lab demonstration.

## The outcome

The students completed a working **prototype** grounded in their own structural testing — an
evidence-based answer to a question BBC is asked often: can a natural material like bamboo really
take the strain? It was an applied-engineering education project, not a commercial commission, and
that is the point: BBC turns real design problems into real teaching.

## In James's words

> "Project with UCL, working with Master students on a 6-month project testing structural
> properties and building a cargo bike to hold 150kg. Our education programme has grown and we
> work with schools in the UK and abroad."

## Why it matters

This is how Bamboo Bicycle Club's education work runs at university level: live, hands-on briefs
where students design, build and test for themselves. It is the same ethos that drives BBC's
school and workshop programmes — learning by making, with the evidence to back it up.
