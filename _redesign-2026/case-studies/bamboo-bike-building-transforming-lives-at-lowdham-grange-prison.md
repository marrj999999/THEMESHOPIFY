# Building bamboo bikes — and brighter futures — at HMP Lowdham Grange

## The context
At HMP Lowdham Grange, a Category B men's training prison in Nottinghamshire, Makers learn to build a complete bicycle from raw bamboo. It is the first prison where Bamboo Bicycle Club delivered this work — the place that proved the idea works, and the model every site since has been built on.

## What we do
Since late 2024 we have run a six-week course in a dedicated on-site workshop:

- 12 Makers per cohort, 18 hours a week
- A clear progression from the first basic joint to a finished frame
- Peer mentors, trained from earlier cohorts, supporting the next group
- A Build to Bond strand running alongside, focused on rebuilding family connection

The course is accredited by OCN London at Level 1 and Level 2 in Sustainable Manufacturing — a recognised qualification Makers can carry with them.

## The outcome
The programme is oversubscribed: more Makers want a place than the workshop can take. Cohorts have completed, peer mentors have stepped up to teach the next intake, and the workshop has become a settled, working part of the prison. Lowdham Grange is where Build to Bond began, and the proof of concept for everything that followed.

## In James's words
"Training people in the building of frames and the mechanics of bikes, so they can build themselves a brighter future." — James Marr, founder, Bamboo Bicycle Club

"The whole goal of Bamboo Bicycle Club is to make bike-building accessible to everyone." — James Marr (Inside Time)

## Why it matters
A bamboo frame is something you can shape with simple hand tools and real patience — and at the end of it, you are holding something you made. For the Makers at Lowdham Grange, that is a skill, a recognised qualification, and, through Build to Bond, a way to reconnect with family. Across our work we have trained 4,000+ people in 36+ countries since 2012; this is where that work began behind the wall.
