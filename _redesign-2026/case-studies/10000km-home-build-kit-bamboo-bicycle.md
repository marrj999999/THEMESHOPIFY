# He Built His Own Frame at Home — and Has Ridden 10,000km on It

One of our customers built a bamboo frame at home from a home build kit — no workshop visit needed — and has since put roughly **10,000km** on it since spring 2022. It is the kind of story we love most: not a one-ride photo, but a bike that has earned its scars.

## What he did
The frame took about two weeks to build, plus a couple of days to fit the components. He mitred the bamboo by hand, set stainless inserts at the head tube, bottom bracket, seat tube and rear dropouts, and bound the joints in natural fibre and two-part epoxy. He is honest about the bits that went wrong along the way — a split seat tube, a resin reaction that ran hotter than expected, a rear axle that needed realigning, and an early finish that he later recoated with boat varnish so it would survive a life outdoors. That honesty is the point: this is what learning to build actually looks like.

## Where it has been
On that one home-built frame he has ridden, among others:

- Edinburgh to the Isle of Mull
- King Alfred's Way
- The Cantii Way
- A roughly 1,600km loop of the Irish coast
- The Norfolk 360
- The Pennine Rally — twice

Every one of those is a real, well-known UK or Ireland bikepacking route or event.

## In his words
> "It's pretty comfortable — bamboo absorbs a lot of shock. You can feel the bike really flexing under you, especially when it's fully loaded for bikepacking."

## Why it matters
A home build kit is not a compromise. With the instructions, the jigs and a bit of patience, someone with no framebuilding background built a bike capable of 10,000km of loaded bikepacking — and learned to fix it when things went sideways. That is the whole idea behind Bamboo Bicycle Club: not selling you a bike, but teaching you to build one you can trust and repair. Since 2012, more than 4,000 people in 36 countries have done exactly that.
