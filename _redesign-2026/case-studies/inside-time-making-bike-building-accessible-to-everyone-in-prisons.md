# Inside Time: Making bike-building accessible to everyone in prisons

In November 2025, the national prison newspaper Inside Time featured Bamboo Bicycle Club's work at HMP Lowdham Grange — under the headline "Bamboozling way to build bikes", written by Ben Leapman.

## The context
HMP Lowdham Grange is a Category B men's prison in Nottinghamshire, home to around 900 residents. It is the first prison where Bamboo Bicycle Club delivered its programme — the site that proved the idea works, and the model every site since has been built on.

## What we do
At Lowdham Grange, six Makers at a time take a six-week course, building a complete bicycle from raw bamboo. The course is oversubscribed, with a waiting list to take part.

- Six Makers per cohort, over six weeks
- A clear progression from the first basic joint to a finished frame
- Three Makers have been trained as peer instructors, supporting the next group
- On completion, Makers earn an OCN London Level 2 certificate in Sustainable Design and Manufacturing

Running alongside the build course is **Build to Bond**, in which a Maker builds a children's bamboo balance bike to give to their own child, grandchild, niece or nephew — a way of rebuilding family connection.

The prison programme is funded on a cost-recovery basis: profits from our £695 public workshops help subsidise the work behind the wall.

## The outcome
The course has become a settled, working part of the prison. Cohorts have completed, peer instructors have stepped up to teach the next intake, and demand outstrips the places available. Lowdham Grange is where Build to Bond began, and the proof of concept for everything that followed.

## In James's words
"The whole goal of Bamboo Bicycle Club is to make bike-building accessible to everyone." — James Marr, founder

"For a lot of prisoners, the programmes offered are quite dull, but bamboo bikes have that cool factor that excites them. In the programme there's maths and English, but it's built into design, creativity and bike mechanics." — James Marr

"I believe building things is powerful, and can help people progress in their lives. A lot of people in prison are disillusioned and have been failed by the education system. I am dyslexic myself, so I can sort of relate." — James Marr

The Prisons Minister, Lord Timpson, backed the scheme: "Innovative projects, such as Build to Bond, support rehabilitation and help people leave prison as better citizens, boosting the economy and keeping our streets safe."

## Why it matters
A bamboo frame is something you can shape with simple hand tools and real patience — and at the end of it, you are holding something you made. For the Makers at Lowdham Grange, that means a skill, a recognised qualification, and, through Build to Bond, a way to reconnect with family. Inside Time reaches every prison in England and Wales, so this is also a story that lands directly with the people it is about. Across our work we have trained 4,000+ people in 36+ countries since 2012; this is where that work began behind the wall.
