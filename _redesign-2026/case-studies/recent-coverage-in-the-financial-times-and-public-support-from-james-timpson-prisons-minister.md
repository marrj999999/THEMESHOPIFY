# As featured in the Financial Times: our prison rehabilitation work, backed by the Prisons Minister

In September 2025, the **Financial Times** featured Bamboo Bicycle Club's prison rehabilitation work in a piece by journalist Marion Willingham, *"How Bamboo Bicycle Club is helping to break the prison cycle."* The same month, **Lord (James) Timpson, the UK Prisons Minister**, publicly backed the programme. National press and government recognition for the same work, in the same month.

## What the programme does

At **HMP Lowdham Grange** in Nottinghamshire we run a six-week course, around four hours a day, from a dedicated 2,000 sq ft on-site workshop. Makers progress from workshop safety and material science through bamboo frame construction, mechanics and finishing — building a real, rideable bicycle while developing teamwork, problem-solving and a genuine sense of pride. The course is accredited through OCN London at Level 1 and Level 2.

The aim is bigger than the bike. As founder James Marr put it to the FT:

> "I don't want everyone to go out and be a bike builder. I want them to be like, 'I'm really interested in the engineering of this, I'm going to do an engineering course,' or 'I'm really interested in the look of this, I'm going to do a graphic design course.'"

## Build to Bond

Alongside the build course runs **Build to Bond**, where Makers build balance bikes for their own children. The point is connection: the bike becomes a reason to rebuild communication and confidence with family — relationships that matter long after release.

## The recognition

> "Innovative projects, such as Build to Bond, support rehabilitation and help people leave prison as better citizens, boosting the economy and keeping our streets safe."
> — Lord (James) Timpson, UK Prisons Minister

From our own coverage of the work on site, **Sally Allsopp, Industries and Community Manager at HMP Lowdham Grange**, has described what the course adds beyond the technical skills:

> "The bamboo bike course offers more than just technical training — it provides an avenue for creativity, teamwork, and self-expression."

This recognition reflects the dedication of our Makers and staff, and the strength of the partnership with the prison's industries team.
