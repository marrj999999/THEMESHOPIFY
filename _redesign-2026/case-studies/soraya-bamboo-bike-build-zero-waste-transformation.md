# Soraya built a bike to break the spell that making is only for geniuses

Soraya didn't come to us to win races. She came to understand her own bike — so that when something broke a long way from home, she'd know how to fix it.

## What happened

Soraya built a bamboo road bike with us at our Canning Town workshop in London, over about four and a half days across two visits. She started in the summer of 2019, then came back a few months later to finish, after a busy stretch tied up with an ocean-plastic expedition she was involved in.

Her reasons were practical and deliberate. She wanted to learn maintenance before heading off on long-distance bikepacking. She wanted expert help choosing components instead of store-bought compromises. And she wanted a lower-impact bike whose materials she could actually trace.

Before all this she'd been a teacher, then spent three years at the Marine Megafauna Foundation in Tofo, Mozambique, working with manta rays, whale sharks and turtles. The thread running through it is the same: pay attention to where things come from.

## What we did

We guided Soraya through the whole build — mitring and wrapping the joints, setting the geometry, choosing parts — rather than handing her a finished machine. She named the bike Erica and added decals, including her website address, so people could follow her on the rides to come.

## Why it matters

The honest story here isn't a spec sheet. It's the shift in what someone believes they're capable of. Soraya put it better than we could:

> "We are so divorced from where everything comes from, we think that building things is a mythical thing that only geniuses can do. When broken down, every step was actually simple and achievable."

That's the whole idea. Broken into steps, building a bike stops being a mystery and becomes something an ordinary, determined person can do.

## In Soraya's words

> "I loved it. It's been a while since I did anything practical with my hands and my brain, rather than working at a laptop."

She called the long hours of sanding the joints "type 2 fun" — hard at the time, satisfying once it was done.
