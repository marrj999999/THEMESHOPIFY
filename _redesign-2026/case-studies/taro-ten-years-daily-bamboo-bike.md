# Ten Years, One Bamboo Bike: Taro Tsuruta's Daily London Commute

> Note: this story first appeared in Taro's own words on our club-news blog. This is the impact-page version. Recommend a canonical/redirect to the original post.

## Context

In 2015, London architect Taro Tsuruta read a Financial Times article about building a bicycle from bamboo. He had a reason to act on it: his mother was living with Alzheimer's, and he wanted to do a charity ride for Alzheimer's research. He came to a Bamboo Bicycle Club workshop, chose his bamboo, and built his own frame.

## What we did

Taro joined a frame-building workshop and built a custom bamboo road frame with us — measured to his body, made by his own hands.

## The verified outcome

A decade later, Taro still rides that same frame every day — around sixteen miles across London to his architecture practice. After ten years of daily commuting, through London rain and traffic, the frame remains structurally sound and has needed very little attention: cables, a Brooks saddle changed four or five times, electrical tape on the cabling every few months. The frame itself, at roughly 10 kg, has carried him on far more than the commute:

- **Dunwich Dynamo** — around 180 km overnight, in about ten hours including rest stops.
- **London to London via Antwerp** — over 600 km on the same frame.

He's now followable on Instagram with a new build underway.

## In Taro's words

> "Even after 10 years, every few days someone stops me to ask about the bike."

On the Dunwich Dynamo, after he'd been mentioned on the LFGSS cycling forum, riders kept calling out as they passed: *"You're the guy with the bamboo bike!"*

His advice to anyone thinking about building their own:

> "Take accurate body measurements and make it as custom as possible. But most importantly — speak to James Marr!"

## Why it matters

The question people always ask about a bamboo bicycle is whether it will last. Taro's answer isn't a promise — it's ten years of daily evidence on London's streets: the same frame, the same routes, still going. A handbuilt bike, made for a reason that mattered, ridden every day since.
