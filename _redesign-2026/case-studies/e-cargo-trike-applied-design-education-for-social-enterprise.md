# When a local delivery firm couldn't afford an e-cargo bike, we designed one

**A neighbour's real problem became the brief: build a bamboo e-cargo trike good enough — and cheap enough — to run a delivery round on.**

## The challenge

A local London delivery company, Loafly, ran their rounds on e-cargo bikes — but the cost of commercial machines was holding their growth back. Bamboo Bicycle Club took that constraint as a design brief and set out to build a flat-pack bamboo e-cargo trike from scratch, designed to be shipped and self-assembled rather than bought off the shelf.

## What we did

We started where any honest design project should: with the existing options. We reviewed commercial cargo bikes, pinned down where they fall short — awkward ergonomics, rigid canopies, cheap bought-in parts — and set clear objectives against them. The result was a frame in 100mm Guadua bamboo with flax connections (no moulds, no welding), built around a 250kg load capacity, a differential all-wheel drive, hydraulic disc brakes and a recyclable, modular honeycomb cargo box. Because the bamboo-and-flax method needs no tooling, we could prototype quickly and keep iterating.

## The outcome

The trike was designed and prototyped as a genuine working concept, and the project drew international press coverage in 2023, from Cycling Electric and InsideEVs among others. The longer-term goal is a pre-fabricated lug kit that cuts build time sharply, so a small business can buy a core chassis and adapt the cargo box to its own round — a target we're still working towards, not a finished production line.

## Why it matters

This is how we think education should work: a real problem, real constraints, real stakes. The same principle runs through our school programmes and our free YouTube build tutorials — the best learning happens when the brief is genuine and the outcome has to actually carry the load.
