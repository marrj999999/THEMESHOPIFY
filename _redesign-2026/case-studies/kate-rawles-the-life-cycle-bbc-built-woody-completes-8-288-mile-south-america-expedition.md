# Kate Rawles: The Life Cycle — Woody and 8,288 miles down the Andes

Environmental writer and cyclist **Dr Kate Rawles** built her own bamboo bike on a Bamboo Bicycle Club course in London — then rode it the length of South America.

Kate cut and built the frame herself from bamboo grown at the **Eden Project in Cornwall**, with the joints wrapped in Yorkshire hemp and a UK-made plant-based resin. She named the bike **Woody** and called it, as far as she knew, "probably the UK's first home-grown bicycle."

Between 2016 and 2018 Kate rode Woody roughly **8,288 miles** down the spine of the **Andes** — from **Cartagena, Colombia to Cape Horn / Ushuaia**, through Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile and Argentina — reaching Ushuaia on **16 February 2018**. The journey, *The Life Cycle*, was about **biodiversity loss**: she met community groups, activists and officials working to protect habitats, and saw first-hand the damage from gold mining and oil drilling.

Through cloud forests, Bolivian salt flats, the Atacama desert, 5,000-metre passes and Patagonian winds, the bamboo frame held up.

> "The final distance of my ride was 8,288 miles, and Woody made it with virtually no mechanical issues at all."
> — Kate Rawles

Kate's account of the ride became the book **_The Life Cycle: 8,000 Miles in the Andes by Bamboo Bike_** (Icon Books, 1 June 2023).

*Kate built and rode Woody herself; the Bamboo Bicycle Club ran the workshop course where she made the frame. Coverage of the expedition includes The Bogota Post (14 July 2017) and University of Cumbria News.*
