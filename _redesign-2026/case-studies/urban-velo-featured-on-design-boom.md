On **26 November 2020**, the international design magazine **Designboom** published a feature on the **Bamboo Urban Mini Velo** — a custom small-wheeled city bike created by London architecture practice **mccloy + muchemwa** (Steve McCloy and Beatrice Muchemwa) in collaboration with Bamboo Bicycle Club.

The article, titled *"carry your portfolio with the 'bamboo urban mini velo' custom city bike"*, showcased a bike built around how architects actually move through a city. Its split frame holds a central cargo compartment sized for an A3 portfolio and a laptop case — no clunky basket, no awkward backpack. The 20-inch wheels are tuned for quick acceleration in traffic.

The frame is a biocomposite dual top-tube design: bamboo, flax-fibre bindings and a plant-based resin. The bindings are sculpted into stiff, bone-like nodes, and the lights are recessed into four protruding stalks — a small zoomorphic flourish on an otherwise lean, strong frame. The brief originated from a competition run by the humanitarian architecture charity **Article 25**, and the first prototype was built in Bamboo Bicycle Club's London workshop.

Speaking to Designboom, Steve McCloy said:

> "there is freedom for creativity when working with bamboo because the tooling is low-tech, safe and accessible."

He added:

> "at mccloy + muchemwa we challenge the conceptualization and the functionality of each design."
