# Karl designed his own cargo e-bike — and learned to weave for it

Karl, in San Diego, wanted a compact front-loader cargo e-bike that would fit on the local trains. So he drew one up himself, taught himself to weave the basket, and came to us for the frame.

## The build

Karl designed the bike in SketchUp, inspired by the German "Muli" compact Long-John layout, and sized it to fit on San Diego's trains. He came to us with that design; we engineered and supplied the bamboo frame to match it — his shape, our frame.

It's a proper build. A 1000W Bafang mid-drive motor runs off two reused 491Wh e-scooter batteries housed in a homemade battery box under the load bay. The drivetrain pairs a 9-speed cassette with a 3-speed Sturmey Archer hub; it stops on Magura MT5e brakes, and rides on an air-sprung SR-Suntour fork and a Brooks Cambium saddle. The cargo basket is hand-woven — and Karl learned to weave specifically for this project. As he put it, the reed is rattan, "so grass, like the bamboo frame."

## Why it matters

This is exactly the kind of build the kit makes possible: someone has an idea no shop sells, designs it themselves, and we give them the frame to make it real. Karl didn't just assemble a bike — he designed it, sourced the parts, and learned a craft (basket-weaving) to finish it. The result is a working cargo e-bike that replaces car journeys around his city.

## A note on this story

Karl documented the whole build publicly himself, with photos and his own commentary, and credited James and the Bamboo Bicycle Club for the frame.

> "Much thanks to James at the Bamboo Bicycle Club. … I designed it on SketchUp. It's inspired by a German Muli design."
> — Karl, San Diego
