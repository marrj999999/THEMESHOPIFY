# Two riders, six countries and 1,620 rice-based meals: the 2 by Bamboo story

Emily and Sam rode the length of South America on bamboo bikes they built themselves — finishing in Cartagena after six countries and a very long mountain range.

## The challenge

Most people who buy a bamboo frame kit ride it to the shops. Emily and Sam pointed theirs at a continent. Riding under the name "2 by Bamboo", their plan was to cross South America from the bottom up — from Ushuaia, through Patagonia, the Andes and the Bolivian highlands — and raise money along the way for Conservation International, a charity working to protect the natural world.

## What we did

The two of them built their own bamboo bicycles before they left — the kind of home build the Bamboo Bicycle Club exists to make possible. A bamboo frame is light, it soaks up vibration, and once it's bonded it's tough. For a trip measured in months and mountain passes, that mattered: the frame is the one part of a touring bike you can't easily replace on the road.

## The outcome

They made it. In their own words, the ride added up to six countries, roughly 11,300km, "a bloody long mountain range", and 1,620 rice-based meals — finishing in Cartagena, on the Caribbean coast of Colombia. They raised funds for Conservation International as they went, using the bikes themselves to start the conversation: a bamboo frame turns heads, and a turned head is a chance to talk about why you're riding.

## In their words

> "After 6 countries, 11,300km, a bloody long mountain range, lots of laughs, a few sobs, unbelievably kind people and 1,620 rice-based meals — we made it across the finish line in Cartagena!"
>
> — Emily and Sam, 2 by Bamboo

## Why it matters

A bamboo bike isn't a novelty. The same frame a beginner bonds together on a weekend can carry two riders across a continent. That's the whole point of what we do — put the skill to build something genuinely capable into ordinary people's hands, and let them decide how far it goes.
