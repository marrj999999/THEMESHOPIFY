# Kate Rawles rode 8,288 miles down the Andes on a bamboo bike she built with us

Eco-adventurer and environmental philosopher Kate Rawles came to our London workshop in 2016 and spent about five days building her own bamboo frame. She named it Woody. Two years later, Woody had carried her the length of South America.

## The build

Kate built Woody at one of our weekend-length workshops, using bamboo grown at the Eden Project in Cornwall. The joints were wrapped in Yorkshire hemp set in a plant-based resin; everything else was standard mountain-bike kit. In her words: "I went to a workshop run by The Bamboo Bicycle Club in London, and spent about five days learning how to build the bike – and building it!"

## The journey

In 2017 Kate set off from Cartagena on Colombia's Caribbean coast and rode south along the spine of the Andes — through Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia and Argentina — reaching Ushuaia at the bottom of Patagonia on 16 February 2018. Thirteen months, 8,288 miles, solo. Her purpose was to find out why biodiversity matters, what is happening to it, and what can be done — meeting community groups, activists and officials along the way.

## The book

Kate's account, *The Life Cycle: 8,000 Miles in the Andes by Bamboo Bike*, was published by Icon Books in June 2023. It pairs adventure writing with a serious look at the biodiversity crisis.

It carries endorsements from:

- **David Shukman**, former BBC News Science Editor: "A gripping read for anyone who cares about what we're doing to the planet and how we can change it."
- **Tim Smit OBE**, co-founder of the Eden Project: "Searing observations focused on our need to protect biodiversity – A tour de force."
- **Jonathon Porritt**, author and campaigner: "An informative, uplifting and truly important book."

## In Kate's words

> "In threatening species and ecosystems, we're tearing down our own life support system." — Kate Rawles, *The Life Cycle*

## Why it matters

Woody was built by hand from UK-grown bamboo and then ridden across two years and a continent. For us it is the clearest proof of what these bikes can do — and of what they can carry beyond a rider: a question worth asking about the living world.
