A School Workshop That Didn't Pack Up and Leave

Most school enrichment programmes follow the same rhythm: an external provider arrives on a Tuesday, delivers something memorable, and is gone by Thursday. The display boards go up. The thank-you emails are sent. Then, gradually, the experience fades into the general noise of school life.

The Oratory School in Reading decided to do something different. When Bamboo Bicycle Club came to talk about their programme, the school didn't book a day visit. They built a permanent workshop.

Since February 2020, The Oratory School has housed a dedicated bamboo bicycle building facility on its grounds — the first permanently installed BBC workshop inside a UK secondary school. Students don't need to travel to London. The jigs are there. The tools are there. The knowledge is there. The programme runs because the school made the decision to own it.

The Challenge: Enrichment That Actually Sticks

The Oratory School is an HMC independent boarding and day school for boys aged 11–18, founded in 1859 in Woodcote, Oxfordshire. What it was looking for — and what many schools at its level are looking for — was enrichment that goes beyond the academic timetable and leaves something tangible behind.

D&T and engineering enrichment presents a specific challenge at the secondary level. Activities need to be genuinely stretching for students who have already covered resistant materials, CAD design, and electronics. They need to produce something real — not a model, not a simulation, but an object that required genuine skill to make.

The Solution: A Permanent In-School BBC Workshop

Working with Bamboo Bicycle Club, The Oratory School set up a dedicated bamboo bicycle building space on site. BBC provided the frame jigs, tools, technical guidance, and initial materials training. The school provided the space and the commitment to make it a lasting part of what the school offers.

The launch event, held on Saturday 29 February 2020, was open to the whole school community: students and parents were both invited to take part.

The programme links directly to the D&T curriculum:

  • KS3/KS4 Design & Technology: Materials selection, manufacturing processes, structural joinery
  • Physics: Material properties, forces, structural stress and strain
  • Chemistry: Composite materials, epoxy resin reactions, natural fibre reinforcement
  • Geography: Bamboo as a global crop, carbon sequestration, sustainable supply chains
  • STEM enrichment: Engineering design iteration, problem-solving, measurement to tolerance

The bamboo used in the frames is a natural composite — one of the highest tensile-strength-to-weight materials available. The flax fibre wrapping and natural resin joinery technique is the same process used in professional aerospace and automotive composite manufacture, adapted for hand-building. Students are not doing craft. They are doing composite engineering.

Outcomes

The Oratory School's decision to establish a permanent workshop rather than a one-off visit speaks directly to the outcomes. Schools do not invest in permanent infrastructure for programmes that didn't work.

BBC has now supported over 4,000 builds across 36+ countries since 2012. The Oratory School is the first UK secondary school to take the programme in-house — establishing a template that other schools are now able to follow. The self-delivery model means that once a school has the tools, the training, and the space, the programme runs without requiring BBC to be present for every session.

For D&T teachers evaluating the programme, the Oratory precedent answers the most important question: can this be embedded, or is it always a one-off? The answer is in a workshop in Oxfordshire that has been running since February 2020.

How Your School Can Do This

Bamboo Bicycle Club works with secondary schools, sixth form colleges, and further education institutions across the UK to design programmes that fit the curriculum, the timetable, and the available space.

The self-delivery model — where BBC trains one or two members of your D&T staff and equips your workshop — is available to schools that want to run the programme independently, year after year. OCN Level 2 in Sustainable Manufacturing is available as a formal qualification pathway for students aged 14+.
