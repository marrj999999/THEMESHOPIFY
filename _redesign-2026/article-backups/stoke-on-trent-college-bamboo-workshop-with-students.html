When Students Build Alongside Artists

In March 2025, something unusual happened in a workshop in Stoke-on-Trent. A group of further education students sat down alongside a local artist. They had one thing in common: bamboo.

The session — a joint workshop run by Bamboo Bicycle Club and Brilliant Bamboo CIC — was not a demonstration. It was a room full of people with different backgrounds and different skill levels, all working with the same material, learning the same techniques, and making something together.

The Challenge: Bridging Curriculum and Community

Further education colleges face a particular challenge in design and craft education. The student cohort is diverse — different ages, different prior experiences — and the curriculum needs to deliver practical skills, industry relevance, and genuine engagement simultaneously.

Sustainability is increasingly central to FE curriculum design. The Department for Education's requirement for schools and colleges to have active Climate Action Plans (from September 2025) shapes what programmes FE colleges are expected to offer. Students need to engage with sustainable materials not as abstract concepts, but as practical skills they have actually exercised.

The Solution: Cross-Sector Workshop with Brilliant Bamboo CIC

Bamboo Bicycle Club partnered with Brilliant Bamboo CIC to deliver the Stoke-on-Trent College workshop in March 2025. The session brought together college students and a local community artist, working side by side on bamboo building techniques.

Curriculum links included:

  • Creative Arts: Material exploration, tactile making, craft tradition
  • Design Technology: Material properties, joinery techniques, structural thinking
  • Sustainability: Bamboo as a renewable material, low-carbon manufacturing
  • Community and citizenship: Cross-sector collaboration, local artist engagement
  • Employability: Practical skills, attention to detail, working to specification

Outcomes

The Stoke-on-Trent College session demonstrates BBC's strength: the programme works across levels, across backgrounds, and across sectors simultaneously. FE students and community artists in the same workshop is not a compromise — it is an enrichment for both groups.

BBC holds OCN Level 2 accreditation in Sustainable Manufacturing. For FE colleges where accredited outcomes are a funding requirement, the BBC programme can be delivered within an OCN-accredited framework, providing formal qualification evidence for students who complete the full programme.
