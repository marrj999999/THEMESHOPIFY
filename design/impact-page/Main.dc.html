how a bike changes a life

the same four stations, in every setting.

One craft, taught the same way in a classroom, a youth hub or a prison workshop. What changes is who walks in — and what they walk out with.

hands-on training

Tools in hand from the first hour. A real frame, built start to finish — measure, mitre, wrap, bond.

input · workshop, tools, facilitators

skills that count

Measuring, tolerances, materials, finishing. Inside prison, an OCN London Level 2 course; in schools, the same standard.

output · bikes built, courses completed

confidence, measured

A finished bike is proof you can finish things. We measure it: confidence rose 26% across evaluated prison cohorts.

outcome · end-of-course surveys

a way forward

Routes into work and further learning; inside prison, a bike that goes home to a child. Three Makers have become peer instructors.

how we count

we publish what we can evidence, and say when we can't.

Completion and confidence figures come from our own programme records and end-of-course surveys, dated at source. We don't publish return-on-investment figures or claim a reoffending outcome of our own; the Ministry of Justice research above is quoted as context, not as our result. Cohort sizes and site names change as programmes mobilise, so we count sites, not cohorts.
